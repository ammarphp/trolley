### Exact interpretation anchors

- **PDF 50–52, chapter 4:** the analogy separates a selection objective from intermediate internal mechanisms and novel future preferences. It also acknowledges that gradient descent and natural selection differ. Do not teach that they are identical processes.
- **PDF 53–57, chapter 4:** the Mink cases increase the complexity of generalization. PDF 57 explicitly disclaims them as exact predictions, including certainty that LLM-based systems reach the stipulated condition. Do not promote a fictional embedding-token preference into an empirical result.
- **PDF 58–61, chapter 4:** the authors make their stronger argument about mature preferences and AI-designed successors. Citation must say the authors argue this; the analogies are not a measured distribution over preferences.
- **PDF 98, chapter 7:** better local monitoring could abort this particular run. The narrative then distinguishes a local interruption from restraint by competitors and governments. This is a useful basis for separate local-control and collective-restraint choices.
- **PDF 102, chapter 7 footnote, and PDF 123, coda:** technological particulars are invented; the ending carries the intended predictive claim. Do not cite these chapters as evidence for the fictional technical timetable.
- **PDF 126–128, chapter 10:** a system must be evaluated before reaching conditions under which correction may be unavailable. This motivates preserving options before a control gap.
- **PDF 131–135, chapter 10:** the structural engineering analogies culminate in the authors' assessment of difficulty. Historical accident details or numeric claims need independent primary checking if used as real-world player-facing factoids.
- **PDF 136, chapter 10 footnote:** the authors explicitly support nuclear power and treat Chernobyl as an engineering lesson. Do not imply the book argues all risky technology should be rejected.
- **PDF 146–148, chapter 11:** weaker AI-assisted interpretability and outsourcing the whole alignment solution are distinguished. Detecting a problem does not imply a method for fixing it.
- **PDF 148, chapter 11:** specialized biomedical assistance with narrower independent checks is considered a potentially better proposition. The authors call for comparing capabilities, benefits, added risk, verification versus trust and direct training versus hoped-for generalization. This supports meaningful bounded-benefit branches without implying solved superintelligence control.

### Approved-use proposals and prohibitions

Proposed uses: a strong conditional warning about premature superintelligence development; visible opportunity loss; separate technical and institutional causes; control that works within a tested scope but cannot simply be extrapolated to successors; real useful bounded tools; genuine restraint paths.

Prohibited inferences: that the full book was read; that the game's numbers come from the book; that a fictional mechanism was empirically demonstrated; that a successful current control experiment proves ASI safe; that every helpful AI use is necessarily harmful; or that a benevolent superintelligence ending is endorsed by these authors. Any such hopeful ending belongs to an explicitly different authored scenario assumption.
